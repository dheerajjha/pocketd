Privacy Policy

Pocketd · Last updated 21 October 2026

Three kinds of request leave your phone: model searches and downloads, which go to Hugging Face; a fixed list of usage events, which go to Mixpanel unless you turn them off; and, for Pocketd Pro, the requests Apple's StoreKit makes to the App Store. Nothing else is sent anywhere. Your conversations, everything the model says back, and anything Pocketd reads from Health, Calendar or Reminders stay on the device and cannot appear in any of them.

The rest of this page is the detail behind those sentences, including the parts that are less tidy than the sentences are.

What Pocketd collects about you

Usage events, and nothing besides. There are twenty-eight of them and the complete list is one readable file, AnalyticsEvent.swift: the app was opened; the intro showed a step or was finished; a model download started, finished, was cancelled or failed; a model loaded or failed to load; a message was sent; an answer got a thumbs up or down; the brief was written or read aloud; the microphone was used to ask; a Today tile was tapped; Calendar, Reminders or Health was switched on or off, or offered in a conversation; a routine was created, ran, or came due when it could not run; the server started or stopped; a client on your Wi-Fi, or the chat page on the phone itself, was answered; a request was refused because the phone was too hot, too low on battery, or already busy.

An event may carry whether this is the first launch, a model's id, a file size in bytes, how far a download got, how long something took, a rounded memory class for the phone (3gb up to 16gb_plus), a reason drawn from a fixed list, which of the two APIs a client spoke, which of Calendar, Reminders or Health was switched on and from where, which step of the intro, which kind of routine ran and from which template it was made, whether a brief was written on its own or on request, a thumbs up or down, and which Today tile was tapped. Apple's built-in model is reported by the id system:apple-intelligence. Alongside those, the analytics SDK attaches the same facts to every event: an identifier for this installation of the app, the app's version and build number, the iOS version, the device model and screen size, whether the phone is on Wi-Fi or which kind of mobile network, a mobile-carrier field that current iOS answers only with a placeholder, and the SDK's own version. Mixpanel is told not to work out a location from the IP address the events arrive from.

These events are on by default. You › Privacy has a switch, Share anonymous usage counts, that turns them off; turning it off also throws away anything queued and not yet sent, and the installation's identifier. The intro's first screen says the switch is there. Debug builds, including any you make yourself from the source, never send them at all.

What an event cannot contain is not a matter of anyone's good intentions. The property names an event is permitted to use are written out by hand in AnalyticsSchema, next to a list of names that are banned outright — prompt, message, transcript, response, url, filename, address, api_key, steps, heart_rate, sleep, calendar, reminder and the rest — and a test in the repository fails the build if any event carries a banned name, or a name that appears on neither list.

What is still true, and is most of why the rest of this page is worth reading:

  • No account, no sign-in, no email address. The name you give Pocketd to greet you is never sent anywhere.
  • Nothing you type, and nothing the model replies, is transmitted to anyone. Inference happens on the phone, whether the model is Apple's built-in one or one you downloaded.
  • No purchase data. Pocketd Pro is bought through Apple's App Store. Pocketd learns whether this Apple Account owns it and, to recognise someone who installed Pocketd before Pro existed, which build was first installed. It stores neither, reads nothing about the payment, and no usage event mentions a purchase.
  • No crash reporting.
  • No advertising and no ad identifier. Pocketd does not link an advertising framework and never asks for permission to track. Usage events are not joined to anything you do in another app or on any website, and nothing is sold or handed to a data broker.
  • Three outside components, and only one of them reports anything: an HTTP server (FlyingFox), the inference runtime (LocalLLMClient, wrapping llama.cpp), and the analytics SDK (Mixpanel), which sends the events above and nothing else. The built-in model and the purchase come from Apple's own frameworks, FoundationModels and StoreKit.

The app's PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy says the same thing to Apple: tracking off, tracking domains empty, and two collected data types — Product Interaction and Device ID — both declared not linked to your identity, not used for tracking, and collected for analytics.

What leaves your phone

Three destinations. huggingface.co, with the CDN it redirects downloads to, for models; api.mixpanel.com, for the usage events above; and Apple's App Store, which StoreKit reaches on Pocketd's behalf for Pocketd Pro. Only these things cause a request:

WhenWhat is sent
You search for a modelThe text you typed, as a search term, and your phone's IP address
You open a repository to see its filesThe repository's name, and your phone's IP address
You download a modelWhich repository and file you are fetching, and your phone's IP address
You use the app, with usage counts onOne of the twenty-eight usage events, the identifier for this installation, the device facts above, and your phone's IP address
Pocketd checks for Pocketd Pro, or you open, buy or restore itWhat StoreKit sends Apple for any app's in-app purchase: the product, and the purchase or restore you asked for. The payment itself is between you and Apple

A paired device on your network can ask the phone to run a search or start a download on its behalf, which sends the same things and nothing more.

Pocketd attaches no account and no token to the first four, because it has neither to attach. The App Store's requests carry your Apple Account, as every purchase on an iPhone does, under Apple's privacy policy rather than this one. The identifier in the usage-events row is one the analytics SDK generates for the installation; it is not the advertising identifier and it is attached to nothing but those twenty-eight events. Every download address is assembled as huggingface.co, then a repository and a filename; the search API accepts a repository and a filename and never a host. The stored record for a model carries a field that could name a different address, and nothing in the app or its HTTP API ever sets it.

Nothing else is contacted. Inference does not call out — Apple's built-in model runs on the phone through the FoundationModels framework, a downloaded model is a file on your phone, and answering a question reads one or the other. The web pages Pocketd serves are self-contained, with no fonts, scripts or images loaded from anywhere.

Health, Calendar and Reminders

If you grant access, Today shows what is next, what is due, how you slept and how active you have been, and the assistant can answer questions about your activity, heart and sleep, your calendar and your reminders.

  • The data is read on this device and is never uploaded. Today and the pages under it read it directly, with no model involved. A question goes to the model on your phone — Apple's built-in one, or a file you downloaded — and comes back as an answer.
  • Network callers cannot reach it. Pocketd serves other devices on your network, and those devices are refused this data — including the chat page Pocketd itself serves, which is treated as a network client like any other. The decision is made from the connection's own peer address, before any read happens, so a refused request causes no read at all rather than a filtered one. Your personal data is shown only on the phone itself: on Today and its pages, in the brief and its widget, in the conversation, and in what your routines found.
  • Health data is never used for advertising or marketing, never sold, and never disclosed to anyone. It cannot ride out in a usage event either: steps, heart_rate, sleep, workout, calendar, reminder and event_title are on the banned list a test enforces, and an event carrying a property name that is not explicitly permitted fails the build.
  • Pocketd never writes to Health. The write permission string exists only because iOS requires one from any app that links HealthKit.
  • Health data is kept out of iCloud Backup. Conversations, the brief, the Today widget's copy of it and your routines are excluded from iCloud Backup, because an answer about your sleep or resting heart rate can be saved in them.

One thing Pocketd cannot tell you: iOS never reports to an app whether a request to read Health was allowed. That is deliberate on Apple's part — it stops an app distinguishing "you said no" from "you have no data of that kind" — and it applies to Pocketd like every other app. The app's own Privacy details screen says so rather than guessing. Settings › Privacy & Security › Health › Pocketd is the only place with the answer.

What is stored on your phone

Almost everything Pocketd keeps stays inside the app's own container:

  • Models you downloaded. Not about you.
  • Conversations — everything you asked and everything the model replied, as plain text, one file each.
  • Routines, and what each one last found.
  • Today's brief, and the facts it was written from.
  • Settings, including the name you gave Pocketd, which model you chose, whether usage counts are on, and the API key other devices use to reach this phone.
  • Queued usage events, held by the analytics SDK until they are sent and deleted once they are.
  • Caches iOS keeps on the app's behalf, and snapshots iOS takes of the app when you switch away.

Two small files live outside it, in the container the app shares with its widgets, because a widget runs in a process of its own and cannot read the app's: the line the Today widget shows — a greeting with your name, the first sentence of the brief, the next event's title and time, and how many reminders are due — and, for the Routines widget, your routines' names, when the next one runs and whether something is waiting, with nothing any routine found. A tap on the Ask Pocketd control also leaves a small marker there — when the tap happened, and that it asked for the microphone — which the app clears when it opens.

Models, conversations, routines and the brief are excluded from iCloud Backup. Privacy details, under You › Privacy, walks the container, counts the widget's copy of the brief alongside it, accounts for every byte, and offers to delete conversations, unfinished downloads, the network cache or everything. Deleting the app removes all of it, the widgets' files included.

The local network

When you turn on Use from your laptop, your phone runs an HTTP server for other devices on your Wi-Fi.

  • The server only answers. It accepts connections and never opens one.
  • It serves only a model you downloaded. Apple's built-in model answers the phone's own questions and is never exposed to another device.
  • While it is running, the phone announces itself over Bonjour to everyone on the same Wi-Fi: the name iOS gives apps for the device (on current versions of iOS, simply "iPhone"), the app version, which APIs it speaks, whether a key is required, and the id of the model it serves. That is how a laptop finds the phone without anyone typing an address. It does not leave the local network, and it stops when the server stops.
  • Requests can require a key, and pairing is a six-digit code shown on the phone. Anything reaching the server still cannot read your Health, Calendar or Reminders — see above.

The awkward parts

Worth saying plainly, because they are real and they are the kind of thing a privacy page usually leaves out:

  • The usage events are on by default. You are not asked during the intro; the intro tells you they exist and where the switch is. You › Privacy has the switch, and turning it off also discards anything queued and not yet sent. On-by-default is a real choice with a real cost — most people never change a default, so most installs will send these events. That is the point of them, and it is why the schema and the test that stand between them and anything you typed matter more than the switch does.
  • Opening a model's page hands the address to Safari, and Directions, on the Your day page, hands the event's location to Maps. What Safari or Maps sends from that point is their business, not Pocketd's.
  • Copying puts things on the system pasteboard — the API key, the server address, an answer, the diagnostics log. If Handoff is on, Universal Clipboard forwards the pasteboard to your other Apple devices through iCloud. That is an iOS feature and applies to anything you copy.
  • Hugging Face sees your IP address when you search or download, as any website does, and its CDN may set cookies that the system's HTTP client stores. Privacy details lists that cache and can delete it.

Children

Pocketd has no account, no sign-up and no social features, and asks nobody their age, because it has no reason to know it. The usage events above, unless they are switched off, describe what the app did rather than who was holding it: no name, no email address, no age, no location, and nothing anyone typed.
