Privacy Policy

Pocketd · Last updated 24 November 2026

Pocketd answers on your iPhone by default. Built into iPhone and downloaded brains process your prompts, conversation history and connected day details on the phone. Neither switches to cloud processing.

Apple Private Cloud Compute is optional. Only if you choose this brain during setup or in You › Brain does iOS send content to Apple's private servers. Apple processes the prompt and relevant context for each reply, brief or routine that uses this brain. That context can include earlier messages in the conversation (even if it began with a local brain), your assistant instructions, and details from Calendar, Reminders or Health that you connected. For example, a morning brief can use your next event, a due reminder, last night's sleep and the name you use for greetings. You can select a local brain again in You › Brain. With any brain, Pocketd's mic turns speech into text on the phone. The cloud brain receives dictated text, not the audio.

A paired laptop uses only downloaded models. Its own prompts and replies travel over your local network. The phone doesn't forward them to Apple or give the laptop access to your conversations or connected sources.

Usage counts

Pocketd sends usage events to Mixpanel unless you turn them off. There are thirty-three of them and the complete list is one readable file, AnalyticsEvent.swift: the app was opened; the intro showed a step or was finished; a model download started, finished, was cancelled or failed; a model loaded or failed to load; a message was sent; an answer got a thumbs up or down; the brief was written or read aloud; the microphone was used to ask; a Today tile was tapped; Calendar, Reminders or Health was switched on or off, or offered in a conversation; a routine was created, ran, or came due when it could not run; the server started or stopped; a client on your Wi-Fi, or the chat page on the phone itself, was answered; a request was refused because the phone was too hot, too low on battery, or already busy; or a Pocketd Pro purchase flow started, completed, was cancelled, remained pending or failed.

An event may carry whether this is the first launch, a model's id, a file size in bytes, how far a download got, how long something took, a rounded memory class for the phone (3gb up to 16gb_plus), a reason drawn from a fixed list, which of the two APIs a client spoke, which of Calendar, Reminders or Health was switched on and from where, which step of the intro, which kind of routine ran and from which template it was made, whether a brief was written on its own or on request, a thumbs up or down, which Today tile was tapped, and on Pro purchase events, whether the flow began in Pocketd or on its App Store page. Purchase events carry no price, product identifier, transaction or Apple Account; their event name gives the outcome. The model id identifies the brain, not the prompt; for example, Apple's on-device model is system:apple-intelligence. Alongside those, the analytics SDK attaches the same facts to every event: an identifier for this installation of the app, the app's version and build number, the iOS version, the device model and screen size, whether the phone is on Wi-Fi or which kind of mobile network, a mobile-carrier field that current iOS answers only with a placeholder, and the SDK's own version. Events also carry a build-environment label: Release builds with a sandbox receipt report sandbox (including TestFlight and App Review), and other Release builds report app_store. Debug builds do not transmit events.

These events are on by default. You › Privacy has a switch, Share anonymous usage counts, that turns them off; turning it off also throws away anything queued and not yet sent, and the installation's identifier. The intro's first screen says the switch is there. Debug builds, including any you make yourself from the source, never send them at all.

What an event cannot contain is not a matter of anyone's good intentions. The property names an event is permitted to use are written out by hand in AnalyticsSchema, next to a list of names that are banned outright — prompt, message, transcript, response, url, filename, address, api_key, steps, heart_rate, sleep, calendar, reminder and the rest — and a test in the repository fails the build if any event carries a banned name, or a name that appears on neither list.

About those counts and Pocketd Pro:

  • No Pocketd account, sign-in or email address. Usage events do not include your greeting name, prompts, replies or connected day data. Switching off usage counts doesn't change which brain answers your questions.
  • Optional usage counts include Pocketd Pro purchase-flow outcomes and whether a flow began in Pocketd or on its App Store page. They contain no price, product identifier, transaction or Apple Account. You can turn these counts off with the same switch as the other usage events. The payment and Apple Account stay with Apple; StoreKit tells Pocketd whether Pro is unlocked.
  • No crash reporting.
  • No advertising and no ad identifier. Pocketd does not link an advertising framework and never asks for permission to track. Usage events are not joined to anything you do in another app or on any website, and nothing is sold or handed to a data broker.
  • Three outside components, and only one of them reports anything: an HTTP server (FlyingFox), the inference runtime (LocalLLMClient, wrapping llama.cpp), and the analytics SDK (Mixpanel), which sends the events above. Apple's FoundationModels framework provides both its on-device model and Private Cloud Compute; StoreKit handles purchases.

The app's PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy declares Product Interaction, Device ID and Purchase History for analytics, none linked to your identity or used for tracking. Tracking is off and the manifest lists no tracking domains.

What leaves your phone

These requests go to different services. Hugging Face (huggingface.co and its download CDN) handles model files, Mixpanel (api.mixpanel.com) handles usage counts, and Apple's App Store handles Pocketd Pro. The optional cloud brain is a separate service provided by Apple.

A paired device on your network can ask the phone to run a search or start a download on its behalf, which sends the same things and nothing more.

Pocketd attaches no account or sign-in token to model searches, downloads or usage events. The App Store's requests carry your Apple Account, as every purchase on an iPhone does, under Apple's privacy policy rather than this one. The identifier in the usage-events row is one the analytics SDK generates for the installation; it is not the advertising identifier and it is attached to nothing but those thirty-three events. Every download address is assembled as huggingface.co, then a repository and a filename; the search API accepts a repository and a filename and never a host. The stored record for a model carries a field that could name a different address, and nothing in the app or its HTTP API ever sets it.

The web pages Pocketd serves to your laptop are self-contained, with no fonts, scripts or images loaded from an outside service.

Health, Calendar and Reminders

If you grant access, Today shows what is next, what is due, how you slept and how active you have been, and the assistant can answer questions about your activity, heart and sleep, your calendar and your reminders.

  • Pocketd reads these sources on your iPhone. The Today tiles and their detail pages read them directly, as do checks such as the Overdue nudge. They don't need a model. When a reply, brief or routine does need a model, your choice in You › Brain determines where the details it uses are processed: on this device, or on Apple's private servers if you selected Apple Private Cloud Compute.
  • Paired devices cannot read these sources. Pocketd serves other devices on your network, and those devices are refused this data — including the chat page Pocketd itself serves, which is treated as a network client like any other. The decision is made from the connection's own peer address, before any read happens, so a refused request causes no read at all.
  • Pocketd does not use Health data for advertising or marketing, or sell it. It cannot ride out in a usage event either: steps, heart_rate, sleep, workout, calendar, reminder and event_title are on the banned list a test enforces, and an event carrying a property name that is not explicitly permitted fails the build.
  • Pocketd never writes to Health. The write permission string exists only because iOS requires one from any app that links HealthKit.
  • Pocketd excludes saved answers from iCloud Backup. Conversations, the brief, the Today widget's copy of it and your routines are excluded from iCloud Backup, because an answer about your sleep or resting heart rate can be saved in them.

One thing Pocketd cannot tell you: iOS never reports to an app whether a request to read Health was allowed. That is deliberate on Apple's part — it stops an app distinguishing "you said no" from "you have no data of that kind" — and it applies to Pocketd like every other app. The app's own Privacy details screen says so rather than guessing. Settings › Privacy & Security › Health › Pocketd is the only place with the answer.

What is stored on your phone

Almost everything Pocketd keeps stays inside the app's own container:

  • Models you downloaded. Not about you.
  • Conversations — everything you asked and everything the model replied, as plain text, one file each.
  • Routines, and what each one last found.
  • Today's brief, and the facts it was written from.
  • Settings, including the name you gave Pocketd, which model you chose, whether usage counts are on, and the API key other devices use to reach this phone.
  • Queued usage events, held by the analytics SDK until they are sent and deleted once they are.
  • Caches iOS keeps on the app's behalf, and snapshots iOS takes of the app when you switch away.

Two small files live outside it, in the container the app shares with its widgets, because a widget runs in a process of its own and cannot read the app's: the line the Today widget shows — a greeting with your name, the first sentence of the brief, the next event's title and time, and how many reminders are due — and, for the Routines widget, your routines' names, when the next one runs and whether something is waiting, with nothing any routine found. A tap on the Ask Pocketd control also leaves a small marker there — when the tap happened, and that it asked for the microphone — which the app clears when it opens.

Pocketd saves conversations on your iPhone whichever brain answered them. Private Cloud Compute processes requests; it isn't a backup for your conversation history. Models, conversations, routines and the brief are excluded from iCloud Backup. Privacy details, under You › Privacy, walks the container, counts the widget's copy of the brief alongside it, accounts for every byte, and offers to delete conversations, unfinished downloads, the network cache or everything. Deleting the app removes all of it, the widgets' files included.

The local network

When you turn on Use from your laptop, your phone runs an HTTP server for other devices on your Wi-Fi.

  • Prompts and replies travel over your Wi-Fi. A paired client can also ask the phone to search for or download a model from Hugging Face.
  • It serves only a model you downloaded. Neither Built into iPhone nor Apple Private Cloud Compute is exposed to a paired device.
  • While it is running, the phone announces itself over Bonjour to everyone on the same Wi-Fi: the name iOS gives apps for the device (on current versions of iOS, simply "iPhone"), the app version, which APIs it speaks, whether a key is required, and the id of the model it serves. That is how a laptop finds the phone without anyone typing an address. It does not leave the local network, and it stops when the server stops.
  • Requests can require a key, and pairing is a six-digit code shown on the phone. Anything reaching the server still cannot read your Health, Calendar or Reminders — see above.

The awkward parts

Worth saying plainly, because they are real and they are the kind of thing a privacy page usually leaves out:

  • The usage events are on by default. You are not asked during the intro; the intro tells you they exist and where the switch is. You › Privacy has the switch, and turning it off also discards anything queued and not yet sent. On-by-default is a real choice with a real cost — most people never change a default, so most installs will send these events. That is the point of them, and it is why the schema and the test that stand between them and anything you typed matter more than the switch does.
  • Opening a model's page hands the address to Safari, and Directions, on the Your day page, hands the event's location to Maps. What Safari or Maps sends from that point is their business, not Pocketd's.
  • Copying puts things on the system pasteboard — the API key, the server address, an answer, the diagnostics log. If Handoff is on, Universal Clipboard forwards the pasteboard to your other Apple devices through iCloud. That is an iOS feature and applies to anything you copy.
  • Hugging Face sees your IP address when you search or download, as any website does, and its CDN may set cookies that the system's HTTP client stores. Privacy details lists that cache and can delete it.

Children

Pocketd has no account, no sign-up and no social features, and asks nobody their age, because it has no reason to know it. The usage events above, unless they are switched off, describe what the app did rather than who was holding it: no name, no email address, no age, no device location, and nothing anyone typed.
